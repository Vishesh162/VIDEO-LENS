# Field notes: the art of noticing

## Summary

Good field notes begin with observation. The lesson uses flowers as a starting point: look for changes in light, movement, and texture before deciding what the scene means. Separate what you can see from what you assume, and keep a record you can return to.

The practice is repeatable: choose one detail, describe it precisely, then compare it with a second observation. Small, specific notes make a stronger record than a general impression.

## Action items

1. Choose a scene and write three specific observations.
2. Keep observations separate from interpretations.
3. Return to the scene and compare your second set of notes.

## Key decisions

1. Record visible details before making an interpretation.
2. Keep both sets of notes so changes can be compared.

## Open questions

1. Which details change when you observe the same scene again?
2. What assumptions did you make before recording the details?
